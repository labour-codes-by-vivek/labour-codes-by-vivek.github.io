Contract labour and inter-State migrant workers

When the contract labour rules apply, the contractor's licence, who pays if the contractor does not, core activities, and migrant workers.

Written from the official text · not reviewed by a lawyer · follows the Central Rules

Key points

  • The contract labour Part of the OSH Code applies to every establishment in which fifty or more contract labour were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months, and to every manpower supply contractor who employed fifty or more s. 45(1).
  • A contractor covered by the Part may not supply or engage contract labour without a licence s. 47(1); a licence is valid for five years for the number of contract labour it states s. 48(3).
  • The contractor may not charge the contract labour any fee or commission, directly or indirectly s. 49.
  • The contractor pays the wages, through bank transfer or electronically s. 55(1)–(2). If the contractor does not pay, or pays short, the principal employer must pay the wages in full or the unpaid balance, and recover it from the contractor s. 55(3).
  • The principal employer provides the Code’s welfare facilities to the contract labour employed in the establishment s. 53.
  • Employing contract labour in the core activities of an establishment is prohibited, except in the cases the Code lists s. 57(1).
  • The inter-State migrant workers Part applies to every establishment in which ten or more inter-State migrant workers were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months s. 59.
  • An inter-State migrant worker is recruited in one State for work in another, or comes from one State and finds work in another, and draws wages of not more than eighteen thousand rupees a month or the higher amount the Central Government notifies s. 2(1)(zf).
  • Once a year the employer pays each inter-State migrant worker a lump sum fare for the journey to their native place and back s. 61.

If you are an employer

  • Use only licensed contractors, and check that contract labour are paid: if they are not, the wages fall on you s. 55(3).

If you are a worker

  • If your contractor does not pay you, the principal employer of the establishment where you work is liable for your wages s. 55(3).

Check your understanding

7 questions. Choose an answer, then open “Show the answer” to check it against the law's own words.

  1. Question 1. The OSH Code's contract labour Part applies to an establishment in which how many contract labour were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifty or more

    The Part applies to every establishment in which fifty or more contract labour were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months s. 45(1)(i).

  2. Question 2. For how long is a contractor's licence under the OSH Code valid?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Five years

    A licence is valid for five years for the number of contract labour it states s. 48(3).

  3. Question 3. May a contractor take a fee or commission from the contract labour?

    Show the answer

    Answer: No: not directly or indirectly, in whole or in part

    The contractor may not charge any fee or commission from the contract labour, directly or indirectly s. 49.

  4. Question 4. A contractor does not pay the contract labour their wages. Who must pay them?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The principal employer, who then recovers the amount from the contractor

    If the contractor fails to pay or pays short, the principal employer must pay the wages in full or the unpaid balance, and recover it from the contractor s. 55(3).

  5. Question 5. Apart from the cases the OSH Code lists, where is employing contract labour prohibited?

    Show the answer

    Answer: In the core activities of the establishment

    Employing contract labour in the core activities of any establishment is prohibited s. 57(1).

  6. Question 6. The inter-State migrant workers Part applies to an establishment in which how many inter-State migrant workers were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Ten or more

    The Part applies to every establishment in which ten or more inter-State migrant workers were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months s. 59.

  7. Question 7. What must the employer pay every inter-State migrant worker in a year?

    Show the answer

    Answer: A lump sum fare for a to and fro journey to their native place

    The employer pays every inter-State migrant worker, in a year, a lump sum amount of fare for a journey to their native place and back s. 61.
