Safety, health and welfare at work

The employer's and the employee's duties, reporting danger and accidents, welfare facilities and when they are needed, and women's working hours.

Written from the official text · not reviewed by a lawyer · follows the Central Rules

Key points

  • Every employer must keep the workplace free from hazards that cause or are likely to cause injury or occupational disease OSH s. 6(1)(a), and provide a free annual health examination or test to the employees the appropriate Government prescribes s. 6(1)(c).
  • Every employee must take reasonable care for their own health and safety and that of others affected by their acts or omissions, and comply with the safety and health standards s. 13(a)–(b).
  • An employee who reasonably fears imminent serious injury, death or danger to health may tell the employer, directly or through the Safety Committee, and at the same time the Inspector-cum-Facilitator s. 14(2).
  • The employer must give notice of an accident that causes death, or an injury that keeps the person from working for forty-eight hours or more s. 10(1).
  • Welfare facilities include separate washing facilities, separate bathing places and locker rooms for male, female and transgender employees, and first-aid boxes s. 24(1).
  • A canteen is needed where one hundred or more workers, including contract labourers, are ordinarily employed s. 24(1)(v).
  • An ambulance room may be required in a factory, mine or building work with more than five hundred workers, and shelters, rest rooms and a lunch room in a factory or mine with more than fifty s. 24(2)(i), (iii).
  • A factory, mine or plantation where two hundred and fifty or more workers are ordinarily employed may be required to appoint a welfare officer s. 24(2)(iv).
  • Safety officers are appointed in a factory with five hundred workers or more, a factory with a hazardous process or building work with two hundred fifty or more, and a mine with one hundred or more s. 22(2).
  • Women may be employed in all establishments for all types of work, and with their consent before 6 a.m. and beyond 7 p.m., subject to the conditions the appropriate Government prescribes s. 43.

If you are an employer

  • Use the checklist to see which welfare facilities and safety posts your headcount brings in s. 24 s. 22(2).

If you are a worker

  • You have the right to get information from your employer about your health and safety at work s. 14(1).

Check your understanding

8 questions. Choose an answer, then open “Show the answer” to check it against the law's own words.

  1. Question 1. From how many workers ordinarily employed, counting contract labourers, must an establishment have a canteen?

    Show the answer

    Answer: One hundred or more

    A canteen is needed in an establishment where one hundred or more workers, including contract labourers, are ordinarily employed s. 24(1)(v).

  2. Question 2. In a factory, mine or building work, from how many workers ordinarily employed may an ambulance room be required?

    Show the answer

    Answer: More than five hundred

    The Central Government may prescribe an ambulance room in every factory, mine or building work where more than five hundred workers are ordinarily employed s. 24(2)(i).

  3. Question 3. An employee reasonably fears imminent serious injury at the workplace. Whom may they tell?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Their employer, and at the same time the Inspector-cum-Facilitator

    The employee may tell the employer, directly or through the Safety Committee, and at the same time bring it to the notice of the Inspector-cum-Facilitator s. 14(2).

  4. Question 4. An injury at work keeps a person from working. From how long an absence must the employer give notice of the accident?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Forty-eight hours or more

    Notice is given of an accident causing death, or an injury that prevents the person from working for forty-eight hours or more s. 10(1).

  5. Question 5. May women be employed before 6 a.m. and beyond 7 p.m. under the OSH Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Yes, with their consent and on the conditions the appropriate Government prescribes

    Women may be employed, with their consent, before 6 a.m. and beyond 7 p.m., subject to the conditions on safety, holidays and working hours the appropriate Government prescribes s. 43.

  6. Question 6. What health examination must an employer provide to the employees the appropriate Government prescribes?

    Show the answer

    Answer: An annual health examination or test, free of cost

    The employer provides an annual health examination or test free of cost to the employees the appropriate Government prescribes s. 6(1)(c).

  7. Question 7. From how many workers must a factory without a hazardous process appoint safety officers?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Five hundred or more

    Safety officers are appointed in a factory with five hundred workers or more s. 22(2)(a).

  8. Question 8. What does the OSH Code require of every employee at the workplace?

    Show the answer

    Answer: To take reasonable care for their own health and safety and that of others

    Every employee must take reasonable care for their own health and safety and that of others who may be affected by their acts or omissions s. 13(a).
