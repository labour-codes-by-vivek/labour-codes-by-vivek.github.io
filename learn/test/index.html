Test yourself

One question from each of the 10 lessons, drawn from 73 in all. Each answer shows the law's own words and links to the provision and to the lesson it comes from.

Questions

10 questions, the first from each lesson. Choose an answer, then open “Show the answer” to check it.

  1. Question 1. Which Code repealed the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The Code on Social Security, 2020

    The Code on Social Security, 2020 lists the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 among the enactments it repeals s. 164(1).

    From the lesson The Labour Codes in brief

  2. Question 1. Which Code repealed the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The Industrial Relations Code, 2020

    The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 repeals the Trade Unions Act, the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act and the Industrial Disputes Act s. 104(1).

    From the lesson The Labour Codes in brief

  3. Question 1. From what date did the Central Government bring most provisions of the Code on Wages into force?

    Show the answer

    Answer: 21 November 2025

    S.O. 5322(E) appointed 21 November 2025 as the date on which most provisions of the Code on Wages came into force S.O. 5322(E).

    From the lesson The Labour Codes in brief

  4. Question 1. Under the Code on Wages, which Government is the appropriate Government for a banking company?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The Central Government

    For banking and insurance companies, railways, mines, major ports and central public sector undertakings, among others, the appropriate Government is the Central Government s. 2(d)(i).

    From the lesson The Labour Codes in brief

  5. Question 1. For a shop that is not in any of the Central Government's categories, which Government is the appropriate Government under the Code on Wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The State Government

    For any establishment outside the Central Government’s categories, the appropriate Government is the State Government s. 2(d)(ii).

    From the lesson The Labour Codes in brief

  6. Question 1. Who makes the rules for carrying out the Code on Wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The appropriate Government

    The appropriate Government makes rules for carrying out the provisions of the Code, after previous publication s. 67(1).

    From the lesson The Labour Codes in brief

  7. Question 1. Besides inspecting establishments, what may an Inspector-cum-Facilitator do under the Code on Wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Give advice to employers and workers on complying with the Code

    An Inspector-cum-Facilitator may advise employers and workers on complying with the Code, and inspect the establishments assigned to them s. 51(5).

    From the lesson The Labour Codes in brief

  8. Question 2. Under the OSH Code, a place where a trade or business is carried on is an “establishment” when how many workers are employed in it?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Ten or more

    A place where any industry, trade, business, manufacturing or occupation is carried on is an establishment if ten or more workers are employed s. 2(1)(v).

    From the lesson Who is covered

  9. Question 2. A manufacturing process is carried on with the aid of power. From how many workers are the premises a “factory” under the OSH Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twenty or more

    With the aid of power, premises are a factory where twenty or more workers are working, or were working on any day of the preceding twelve months s. 2(1)(w).

    From the lesson Who is covered

  10. Question 2. Under the OSH Code, a person employed in a supervisory capacity is not a “worker” if their wage is more than how much a month, unless the Central Government notifies another amount?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹18,000

    The definition of worker leaves out a supervisor drawing wages above eighteen thousand rupees a month or the amount the Central Government notifies s. 2(1)(zzl).

    From the lesson Who is covered

  11. Question 2. The Employees' Provident Fund Chapter of the Code on Social Security applies to every establishment employing how many employees?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twenty or more

    The Employees’ Provident Fund Chapter applies to every establishment in which twenty or more employees are employed First Schedule.

    From the lesson Who is covered

  12. Question 2. The Employees' State Insurance Chapter applies to every establishment in which how many persons are employed (a seasonal factory aside)?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Ten or more

    The Employees’ State Insurance Chapter applies to every establishment in which ten or more persons are employed, other than a seasonal factory First Schedule.

    From the lesson Who is covered

  13. Question 2. From how many workers must an industrial establishment have a Grievance Redressal Committee?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twenty or more

    Every industrial establishment employing twenty or more workers must have one or more Grievance Redressal Committees s. 4(1).

    From the lesson Who is covered

  14. Question 2. Whom does the Code on Wages definition of “employee” cover?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Managerial and administrative staff as well as manual and clerical workers

    An employee is anyone employed on wages to do skilled, semi-skilled or unskilled, manual, operational, supervisory, managerial, administrative, technical or clerical work s. 2(k).

    From the lesson Who is covered

  15. Question 3. Which of these does the definition of wages in the Code on Wages include?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Dearness allowance

    Wages include basic pay, dearness allowance and any retaining allowance s. 2(y).

    From the lesson What counts as wages

  16. Question 3. Which of these does the definition of wages leave out?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Gratuity payable on termination of employment

    Gratuity payable on termination of employment is among the payments the definition of wages leaves out s. 2(y)(j).

    From the lesson What counts as wages

  17. Question 3. Excluded payments are added back to wages to the extent they are more than what share of all remuneration?

    Show the answer

    Answer: One-half

    If the excluded payments in clauses (a) to (i) exceed one-half of all remuneration, the amount above one-half is added to wages s. 2(y), first proviso.

    From the lesson What counts as wages

  18. Question 3. An employee gets ₹20,000 a month in basic pay and dearness allowance, and ₹30,000 in excluded allowances. What are their wages under the Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹25,000

    Total remuneration is ₹50,000. Excluded payments above one-half of it, ₹5,000, are added back to the ₹20,000 s. 2(y), first proviso.

    From the lesson What counts as wages

  19. Question 3. Up to what share of total wages does remuneration in kind count as wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifteen per cent

    Remuneration in kind that does not exceed fifteen per cent of the total wages counts as part of the wages s. 2(y), Explanation.

    From the lesson What counts as wages

  20. Question 3. For which purposes are house rent allowance and overtime allowance taken into account as wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Equal wages for all genders and payment of wages

    For equal wages to all genders and for payment of wages, conveyance allowance, house rent allowance, award remuneration and overtime allowance are taken into account s. 2(y), second proviso.

    From the lesson What counts as wages

  21. Question 4. Who notifies the minimum rate of wages that an employer must pay at least?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The appropriate Government

    No employer may pay an employee less than the minimum rate of wages the appropriate Government notifies s. 5.

    From the lesson Minimum wages and paying wages

  22. Question 4. Which Government fixes the floor wage, below which no minimum rate may be set?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The Central Government

    The Central Government fixes the floor wage, taking into account minimum living standards of a worker s. 9(1).

    From the lesson Minimum wages and paying wages

  23. Question 4. At what interval must the appropriate Government ordinarily review or revise minimum rates of wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Not more than five years

    Minimum rates are reviewed or revised ordinarily at an interval not exceeding five years s. 8(4).

    From the lesson Minimum wages and paying wages

  24. Question 4. An employee is paid by the month. By when must the employer pay the wages for a month?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Before the end of the seventh day of the following month

    Monthly wages are due before the expiry of the seventh day of the succeeding month s. 17(1)(iv).

    From the lesson Minimum wages and paying wages

  25. Question 4. An employee resigns. Within how long must the wages payable to them be paid?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Within two working days of the resignation

    Wages are paid within two working days of a removal, dismissal, retrenchment or resignation s. 17(2).

    From the lesson Minimum wages and paying wages

  26. Question 4. What is the most that all deductions together may take from an employee's wages in a wage period?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifty per cent of the wages

    The total of the deductions in any wage period may not exceed fifty per cent of the wages s. 18(3).

    From the lesson Minimum wages and paying wages

  27. Question 4. To remove a difference in pay between women and men doing the same work, may an employer lower the higher wage?

    Show the answer

    Answer: No: the employer may not reduce the rate of wages of any employee to comply

    An employer may not reduce the rate of wages of any employee to comply with the rule against discrimination on the ground of gender in wages s. 3(2)(i).

    From the lesson Minimum wages and paying wages

  28. Question 5. Under the OSH Code, a worker may not be required or allowed to work for more than how many hours in a day?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Eight

    No worker may be required or allowed to work more than eight hours in a day OSH s. 25(1)(a).

    From the lesson Hours, overtime and leave

  29. Question 5. At what rate must overtime work be paid under the OSH Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twice the rate of wages

    Overtime is paid at twice the rate of wages OSH s. 27.

    From the lesson Hours, overtime and leave

  30. Question 5. May an employer require a worker to work overtime?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Only with the worker's consent

    A worker is required to work overtime only subject to their consent OSH s. 27, proviso.

    From the lesson Hours, overtime and leave

  31. Question 5. Under the Central Rules, what is the most overtime a worker may work in a quarter of a year?

    Show the answer

    Answer: 144 hours

    No worker may work overtime exceeding one hundred forty-four hours in any quarter of a year rule 69, proviso.

    From the lesson Hours, overtime and leave

  32. Question 5. A worker paid ₹26,000 a month works overtime of 60 minutes, 120 minutes and 20 minutes on different days. Under the Central Rules, what is the overtime pay?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹875

    The daily wage of a monthly-paid worker is 1/26th of the monthly wages, ₹1,000; over an eight-hour day that is ₹125 an hour, and overtime at twice the rate is ₹250 an hour. The 20 minutes count as thirty minutes, so 3.5 hours are paid: ₹875 rule 69(1)–(3).

    From the lesson Hours, overtime and leave

  33. Question 5. How many days must a worker have worked in a calendar year to be entitled to annual leave with wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: One hundred and eighty days

    A worker who has worked one hundred and eighty days or more in a calendar year is entitled to annual leave with wages s. 32(1)(i).

    From the lesson Hours, overtime and leave

  34. Question 5. At what rate does an adult worker above ground earn annual leave?

    Show the answer

    Answer: A day for every twenty days of work

    Leave is earned at one day for every twenty days of work; adolescent workers and workers below ground in a mine earn it for every fifteen days s. 32(1)(ii).

    From the lesson Hours, overtime and leave

  35. Question 5. How many days of unused annual leave may be carried forward to the next year?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Up to thirty days

    The leave carried forward to a succeeding year may not exceed thirty days s. 32(1)(vii)(a).

    From the lesson Hours, overtime and leave

  36. Question 6. Ordinarily, after how much continuous service is gratuity payable when employment ends?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Not less than five years

    Gratuity is payable on termination after continuous service of not less than five years s. 53(1).

    From the lesson Social security

  37. Question 6. An employee dies after three years of continuous service. Is gratuity payable?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Yes: five years of continuous service are not needed when employment ends on death

    Five years of continuous service are not needed where employment ends on death or disablement or on the expiry of fixed-term employment s. 53(1), second proviso.

    From the lesson Social security

  38. Question 6. For each completed year of service, how many days' wages does gratuity give?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifteen days' wages

    Gratuity is paid at the rate of fifteen days’ wages for every completed year of service s. 53(2).

    From the lesson Social security

  39. Question 6. An employee whose wages last drawn were ₹52,000 a month resigns after 7 years and 8 months of continuous service. How much gratuity is payable?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹2,40,000

    A day’s wage is the monthly wage divided by twenty-six, ₹2,000, so fifteen days’ wages are ₹30,000 a year. The 8 months are more than six, so 8 years are counted: ₹2,40,000 s. 53.

    From the lesson Social security

  40. Question 6. How many days must a woman have actually worked for her employer in the twelve months before her expected delivery to be entitled to maternity benefit?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Not less than eighty days

    She must have actually worked for not less than eighty days in the twelve months before her expected delivery s. 60(2).

    From the lesson Social security

  41. Question 6. For a woman with fewer than two surviving children, what is the maximum period of maternity benefit?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twenty-six weeks

    The maximum period is twenty-six weeks, of which not more than eight weeks may come before the expected delivery s. 60(3).

    From the lesson Social security

  42. Question 6. Who is a “gig worker” under the Code on Social Security?

    Show the answer

    Answer: A person who works and earns outside a traditional employer-employee relationship

    A gig worker performs work or takes part in a work arrangement and earns from it outside a traditional employer-employee relationship s. 2(35).

    From the lesson Social security

  43. Question 6. An aggregator's contribution for gig and platform workers may not exceed what share of the amount it pays or owes those workers?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Five per cent

    The contribution by an aggregator may not exceed five per cent of the amount it pays or owes gig workers and platform workers s. 114(4), proviso.

    From the lesson Social security

  44. Question 7. The OSH Code's contract labour Part applies to an establishment in which how many contract labour were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifty or more

    The Part applies to every establishment in which fifty or more contract labour were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months s. 45(1)(i).

    From the lesson Contract labour and inter-State migrant workers

  45. Question 7. For how long is a contractor's licence under the OSH Code valid?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Five years

    A licence is valid for five years for the number of contract labour it states s. 48(3).

    From the lesson Contract labour and inter-State migrant workers

  46. Question 7. May a contractor take a fee or commission from the contract labour?

    Show the answer

    Answer: No: not directly or indirectly, in whole or in part

    The contractor may not charge any fee or commission from the contract labour, directly or indirectly s. 49.

    From the lesson Contract labour and inter-State migrant workers

  47. Question 7. A contractor does not pay the contract labour their wages. Who must pay them?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The principal employer, who then recovers the amount from the contractor

    If the contractor fails to pay or pays short, the principal employer must pay the wages in full or the unpaid balance, and recover it from the contractor s. 55(3).

    From the lesson Contract labour and inter-State migrant workers

  48. Question 7. Apart from the cases the OSH Code lists, where is employing contract labour prohibited?

    Show the answer

    Answer: In the core activities of the establishment

    Employing contract labour in the core activities of any establishment is prohibited s. 57(1).

    From the lesson Contract labour and inter-State migrant workers

  49. Question 7. The inter-State migrant workers Part applies to an establishment in which how many inter-State migrant workers were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Ten or more

    The Part applies to every establishment in which ten or more inter-State migrant workers were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months s. 59.

    From the lesson Contract labour and inter-State migrant workers

  50. Question 7. What must the employer pay every inter-State migrant worker in a year?

    Show the answer

    Answer: A lump sum fare for a to and fro journey to their native place

    The employer pays every inter-State migrant worker, in a year, a lump sum amount of fare for a journey to their native place and back s. 61.

    From the lesson Contract labour and inter-State migrant workers

  51. Question 8. Under the OSH Code, what must an employer issue to every employee on appointment?

    Show the answer

    Answer: A letter of appointment

    The employer must issue a letter of appointment to every employee on appointment, with the information and in the form the appropriate Government prescribes s. 6(1)(f).

    From the lesson Joining, discipline and leaving

  52. Question 8. From how many workers does an industrial establishment need standing orders under the IR Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Three hundred or more

    The standing orders Chapter applies to every industrial establishment with three hundred or more workers, now or on any day of the preceding twelve months s. 28(1).

    From the lesson Joining, discipline and leaving

  53. Question 8. A worker is suspended pending an inquiry into misconduct. Within what period should the inquiry ordinarily be completed?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Ninety days from the date of suspension

    The investigation and inquiry are to be completed ordinarily within ninety days from the date of suspension s. 38(1).

    From the lesson Joining, discipline and leaving

  54. Question 8. What subsistence allowance is a suspended worker paid for the first ninety days of suspension?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifty per cent of wages

    The allowance is fifty per cent of the wages for the first ninety days of suspension s. 38(3)(a).

    From the lesson Joining, discipline and leaving

  55. Question 8. What notice must be given before retrenching a worker with at least a year of continuous service?

    Show the answer

    Answer: One month's notice in writing with the reasons, or wages for the notice period

    The worker must be given one month’s notice in writing indicating the reasons, or be paid wages for the period of the notice s. 70(a).

    From the lesson Joining, discipline and leaving

  56. Question 8. A worker with average pay of ₹26,000 a month is retrenched after 9 years and 7 months of continuous service. Taking a day's average pay as the monthly figure divided by 26, what is the retrenchment compensation, leaving notice pay aside?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹1,50,000

    Compensation is fifteen days’ average pay for every completed year or part of a year over six months, so 10 years count; at ₹1,000 a day that is ₹1,50,000 s. 70(b). The divisor 26 is an assumption, as the Code does not fix it.

    From the lesson Joining, discipline and leaving

  57. Question 8. For every retrenched worker, how much does the employer contribute to the worker re-skilling fund?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifteen days' wages last drawn

    The employer contributes an amount equal to fifteen days’ wages last drawn for every retrenched worker s. 83(2)(a).

    From the lesson Joining, discipline and leaving

  58. Question 8. Which of these is “retrenchment” under the IR Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Termination by the employer for any reason whatsoever, otherwise than as a punishment

    Retrenchment is termination by the employer for any reason whatsoever, otherwise than as a disciplinary punishment; voluntary retirement and superannuation are not included s. 2(zh).

    From the lesson Joining, discipline and leaving

  59. Question 9. From how many workers ordinarily employed, counting contract labourers, must an establishment have a canteen?

    Show the answer

    Answer: One hundred or more

    A canteen is needed in an establishment where one hundred or more workers, including contract labourers, are ordinarily employed s. 24(1)(v).

    From the lesson Safety, health and welfare at work

  60. Question 9. In a factory, mine or building work, from how many workers ordinarily employed may an ambulance room be required?

    Show the answer

    Answer: More than five hundred

    The Central Government may prescribe an ambulance room in every factory, mine or building work where more than five hundred workers are ordinarily employed s. 24(2)(i).

    From the lesson Safety, health and welfare at work

  61. Question 9. An employee reasonably fears imminent serious injury at the workplace. Whom may they tell?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Their employer, and at the same time the Inspector-cum-Facilitator

    The employee may tell the employer, directly or through the Safety Committee, and at the same time bring it to the notice of the Inspector-cum-Facilitator s. 14(2).

    From the lesson Safety, health and welfare at work

  62. Question 9. An injury at work keeps a person from working. From how long an absence must the employer give notice of the accident?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Forty-eight hours or more

    Notice is given of an accident causing death, or an injury that prevents the person from working for forty-eight hours or more s. 10(1).

    From the lesson Safety, health and welfare at work

  63. Question 9. May women be employed before 6 a.m. and beyond 7 p.m. under the OSH Code?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Yes, with their consent and on the conditions the appropriate Government prescribes

    Women may be employed, with their consent, before 6 a.m. and beyond 7 p.m., subject to the conditions on safety, holidays and working hours the appropriate Government prescribes s. 43.

    From the lesson Safety, health and welfare at work

  64. Question 9. What health examination must an employer provide to the employees the appropriate Government prescribes?

    Show the answer

    Answer: An annual health examination or test, free of cost

    The employer provides an annual health examination or test free of cost to the employees the appropriate Government prescribes s. 6(1)(c).

    From the lesson Safety, health and welfare at work

  65. Question 9. From how many workers must a factory without a hazardous process appoint safety officers?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Five hundred or more

    Safety officers are appointed in a factory with five hundred workers or more s. 22(2)(a).

    From the lesson Safety, health and welfare at work

  66. Question 9. What does the OSH Code require of every employee at the workplace?

    Show the answer

    Answer: To take reasonable care for their own health and safety and that of others

    Every employee must take reasonable care for their own health and safety and that of others who may be affected by their acts or omissions s. 13(a).

    From the lesson Safety, health and welfare at work

  67. Question 10. What register must every employer keep under the Code on Wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: A register of the persons employed, the muster roll and wages

    The employer maintains a register of the persons employed, the muster roll, wages and the other details prescribed s. 50(1).

    From the lesson Records, returns and inspection

  68. Question 10. Which of these must the notice on the establishment's notice board show?

    Show the answer

    Answer: The name and address of the Inspector-cum-Facilitator having jurisdiction

    The notice shows an abstract of the Code, wage rates, the wage period, the time of payment, and the name and address of the Inspector-cum-Facilitator s. 50(2).

    From the lesson Records, returns and inspection

  69. Question 10. The register, notice and wage slip rules do not apply to an employer who employs at most how many persons for agriculture or domestic purposes?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Five

    They do not apply to an employer to the extent they employ not more than five persons for agriculture or domestic purposes s. 50(4).

    From the lesson Records, returns and inspection

  70. Question 10. Within how long must the employer of a new establishment under the OSH Code apply for its registration?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Within sixty days

    The employer applies electronically within sixty days from the date the Code applies to the establishment s. 3(1).

    From the lesson Records, returns and inspection

  71. Question 10. Under the OSH Central Rules, by when must the annual return reach the Inspector-cum-Facilitator?

    Show the answer

    Answer: On or before the last day of February following the calendar year

    The annual return must reach the Inspector-cum-Facilitator on or before the last day of February following the end of each calendar year rule 74.

    From the lesson Records, returns and inspection

  72. Question 10. What is the most an employer can be fined for not maintaining records, or maintaining them improperly, under the Code on Wages?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹10,000

    Non-maintenance or improper maintenance of records is punishable with a fine of up to ten thousand rupees s. 54(2).

    From the lesson Records, returns and inspection

  73. Question 10. Under the Code on Wages (Central) Rules, when must a wage slip be issued?

    Show the answer

    Answer: On or before payment of wages

    The employer issues wage slips in Form V, electronically or on paper, on or before payment of wages rule 52.

    From the lesson Records, returns and inspection
