Social security

Gratuity, maternity benefit and the medical bonus, crèches, and social security for gig and platform workers.

Written from the official text · not reviewed by a lawyer · follows the Central Rules

Key points

  • Gratuity is payable when employment ends after continuous service of not less than five years s. 53(1); the five years are not needed when employment ends on death or disablement or when fixed-term employment expires s. 53(1), second proviso.
  • It is fifteen days’ wages for every completed year of service, or part of a year over six months, at the rate of wages last drawn s. 53(2). For a monthly-rated employee, fifteen days’ wages are the monthly wage divided by twenty-six and multiplied by fifteen s. 53, Explanation 3.
  • The employer must pay gratuity within thirty days from the date it becomes payable s. 56(3).
  • A woman who has actually worked for the employer for not less than eighty days in the twelve months before her expected delivery is entitled to maternity benefit s. 60(2), for up to twenty-six weeks, of which not more than eight weeks come before the expected delivery s. 60(3).
  • With two or more surviving children the maximum is twelve weeks s. 60(3), proviso.
  • A woman who legally adopts a child below the age of three months, or a commissioning mother, is entitled to twelve weeks from the date the child is handed over s. 60(4).
  • If the employer does not provide pre-natal confinement and post-natal care free of charge, she also gets a medical bonus of three thousand five hundred rupees, or the amount the Central Government notifies s. 64.
  • An establishment under the maternity benefit Chapter in which fifty employees, or the number the Central Government prescribes, are employed must have a crèche s. 67(1).
  • A gig worker earns from work outside a traditional employer-employee relationship s. 2(35). Aggregators contribute to schemes for gig and platform workers at a rate the Central Government notifies of not more than two per cent of annual turnover, and never more than five per cent of what they pay those workers s. 114(4).

If you are an employer

  • Work gratuity out on the wages last drawn and pay it within thirty days of its falling due s. 53(2) s. 56(3).

If you are a worker

  • Fixed-term employees do not need five years of service for gratuity; it is paid pro rata s. 53.

Check your understanding

8 questions. Choose an answer, then open “Show the answer” to check it against the law's own words.

  1. Question 1. Ordinarily, after how much continuous service is gratuity payable when employment ends?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Not less than five years

    Gratuity is payable on termination after continuous service of not less than five years s. 53(1).

  2. Question 2. An employee dies after three years of continuous service. Is gratuity payable?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Yes: five years of continuous service are not needed when employment ends on death

    Five years of continuous service are not needed where employment ends on death or disablement or on the expiry of fixed-term employment s. 53(1), second proviso.

  3. Question 3. For each completed year of service, how many days' wages does gratuity give?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Fifteen days' wages

    Gratuity is paid at the rate of fifteen days’ wages for every completed year of service s. 53(2).

  4. Question 4. An employee whose wages last drawn were ₹52,000 a month resigns after 7 years and 8 months of continuous service. How much gratuity is payable?

    Show the answer

    Answer: ₹2,40,000

    A day’s wage is the monthly wage divided by twenty-six, ₹2,000, so fifteen days’ wages are ₹30,000 a year. The 8 months are more than six, so 8 years are counted: ₹2,40,000 s. 53.

  5. Question 5. How many days must a woman have actually worked for her employer in the twelve months before her expected delivery to be entitled to maternity benefit?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Not less than eighty days

    She must have actually worked for not less than eighty days in the twelve months before her expected delivery s. 60(2).

  6. Question 6. For a woman with fewer than two surviving children, what is the maximum period of maternity benefit?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Twenty-six weeks

    The maximum period is twenty-six weeks, of which not more than eight weeks may come before the expected delivery s. 60(3).

  7. Question 7. Who is a “gig worker” under the Code on Social Security?

    Show the answer

    Answer: A person who works and earns outside a traditional employer-employee relationship

    A gig worker performs work or takes part in a work arrangement and earns from it outside a traditional employer-employee relationship s. 2(35).

  8. Question 8. An aggregator's contribution for gig and platform workers may not exceed what share of the amount it pays or owes those workers?

    Show the answer

    Answer: Five per cent

    The contribution by an aggregator may not exceed five per cent of the amount it pays or owes gig workers and platform workers s. 114(4), proviso.
