Provident fund, pension and insurance

Who must join, what the employer and employee pay, when it is due, and how money can be taken out under the Code on Social Security, 2020 and the Schemes of 2026.

Last checked 1 Nov 2026 · written from the official text · not reviewed by a lawyer · follows the Central Rules

In short

  • Provident fund applies to every establishment in which twenty or more employees are employed First Schedule.
  • The employer and the employee each pay twelve per cent of wages S.O. 3582(E).
  • Contributions stop at the wage ceiling, which is ₹25,000 a month S.O. 5109(E).
  • Out of the employer’s share, eight and one-third per cent of wages goes to the Pension Fund S.O. 3580(E).
  • The employer pays a further one-half per cent of wages to the Insurance Fund S.O. 3581(E).
  • Pay within fifteen days of the close of each month G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 28.
  • The employer’s share cannot be taken from the employee’s wages G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 21, and wages cannot be cut because of the employer’s liability to contribute s. 124.

Does this apply to you?

  • The provident fund Chapter applies to every establishment in which twenty or more employees are employed First Schedule.
  • If the employer and most employees agree, the Central Provident Fund Commissioner may apply it to a smaller establishment s. 1(5).
  • The Central Government may also apply the Code to an establishment after two months’ notice s. 1(6).
  • It does not apply to a co-operative society employing less than fifty persons and working without power s. 20(1)(a).
  • It does not apply to a Government establishment, or an establishment set up under a law, whose employees have a contributory provident fund or old age pension under a scheme or rule of its own s. 20(1)(b)–(c).

What the law requires

Who must join

  • An employee becomes a member from the day the Scheme applies to the establishment or the date of joining, whichever is later G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 9.
  • An existing member under the earlier Scheme is a member of the new Scheme G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 9.
  • An employee whose wage is above the ceiling when otherwise entitled to join is an “excluded employee” G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 2.
  • An excluded employee and the employer may jointly choose in writing to enrol the employee; the employer then pays the administrative charges and follows all the statutory provisions G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 9.
  • An apprentice is not an employee under the Code s. 2(26).

How much

  • The employer pays twelve per cent of wages and the employee pays an equal amount G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 18.
  • That rate is deemed to apply from 21 November 2025 S.O. 3582(E).
  • The notification leaves some establishments out of the twelve per cent rate: those with a resolution or repayment plan approved under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, and those in the jute, beedi or brick industry, the coir industry other than spinning, or guar gum factories S.O. 3582(E).
  • The rate for those is ten per cent of wages s. 16(1)(a).
  • Work out contributions on wages actually drawn or payable in the month, rounded to the nearest rupee G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 18.
  • Wages under this Code means basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. If the excluded payments exceed one-half of total remuneration, the excess is added back s. 2(88). The Code on Wages has the same rule, and this calculator works it out.
  • Where wages exceed the ceiling, both contributions are limited to what is payable on the ceiling G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 18 G.S.R. 703(E).
  • An employee may choose to pay more on wages above the ceiling. You need not match it, but you pay extra administrative charges on those wages G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 19.

Example An employee’s wages are ₹30,000 a month, so contributions stop at ₹25,000. The employee pays 12% = ₹3,000 and the employer pays ₹3,000. Of the employer’s share, 8⅓% of ₹25,000 = ₹2,083 goes to the Pension Fund and ₹917 stays in provident fund. The employer also pays ₹125 to the Insurance Fund, and administrative charges.

Pension

  • From the employer’s contribution, send eight and thirty-three hundredths per cent of wages, up to the wage ceiling, to the Pension Fund G.S.R. 527(E), paragraph 4.
  • The Central Government adds its own contribution, limited to wages up to ₹15,000 a month from 17 September 2026. The limit on the employer’s share stays at the wage ceiling G.S.R. 890(E).
  • The Pension Scheme covers an employee who joins the provident fund with wages at or below the ceiling, and a member of the earlier pension Schemes G.S.R. 527(E), paragraph 7.

Insurance

  • The employer’s contribution to the Insurance Fund is worked out on wages up to the ceiling G.S.R. 526(E), paragraph 5.
  • It cannot be taken from the employee’s wages G.S.R. 526(E), paragraph 8.

Deducting the employee’s share

  • Deduct the employee’s contribution from the wages before paying them G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 28.
  • Deduct it only from wages for the period the contribution is for G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 22.
  • A deduction missed by accident or clerical error may be made later from wages with the written consent of the Inspector-cum-Facilitator G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 22.

Workers through a contractor

  • The principal employer is responsible for paying contributions for employees engaged through a contractor G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 20.
  • The contractor is jointly and severally responsible with the principal employer G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 27.
  • The employer may recover the amount from the contractor s. 17(1).

If payment is late

  • Simple interest runs at twelve per cent a year from the date the amount fell due S.O. 2698(E).
  • Damages may also be levied, at the monthly percentage of arrears the Scheme sets for the length of the default, and not more than the arrears G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 23.
  • Late returns carry a fee of ₹500 a day, up to the administrative charges for that month G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 29.
  • Failing to pay, deducting the employer’s share from wages, or failing to file a return are offences s. 133.
  • An appeal against an order on dues or damages goes to the Tribunal s. 23(1).

Records and deadlines

  • Within fifteen days of the Scheme applying to the establishment, send the Commissioner a consolidated return in Form V, or a nil return if no employee is required or entitled to join G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 24.
  • Within fifteen days of the close of each month, upload on the portal the employees who joined and left, and the contribution details in the Electronic Challan-cum-Return in Form VII G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 24.
  • File the ownership return in Form VI, report changes within fifteen days, and display an extract at the entrance and on the website G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 24.
  • A principal employer declares its contractors in Form X, and sends the monthly abstract in Form XII within twenty days of the close of the month G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 27.
  • A claim that cannot be filed online comes to you: verify it and forward it within five days G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 54.
  • Dues are determined by an Authorised Officer, and no proceeding can start more than five years after the amount was due s. 125(1).

Not covered here

  • An establishment running its own provident fund under an exemption s. 143.
  • International workers, who have their own paragraphs in the Scheme G.S.R. 525(E).
  • The later corrections to the Schemes, which change many words and forms. Read them with each Scheme G.S.R. 703(E) G.S.R. 704(E) G.S.R. 705(E).

In short

  • If twenty or more employees work at your workplace, you should be in the provident fund First Schedule.
  • Twelve per cent of your wages is taken from your pay, and your employer adds the same amount from its own money G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 18.
  • Your employer cannot make you pay its share G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 21.
  • Part of your employer’s share goes towards your pension, and your employer also pays for life cover S.O. 3580(E) S.O. 3581(E).
  • You can take out part of your money for illness, education, marriage or a home, usually after twelve months as a member G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 46.
  • Name who should get your money if you die G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 44.

Does this apply to you?

  • Yes, if your workplace employs twenty or more employees First Schedule, unless the law leaves it out.
  • You join from your first day, or from the day the Scheme applies to your workplace if that is later G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 9.
  • If you were already a member under the earlier Scheme, you are a member of the new one G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 9.
  • If your wage is above ₹25,000 a month when you would otherwise join, you need not be a member G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 2 S.O. 5109(E). You and your employer may jointly choose in writing to enrol you G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 9.
  • Workers through a contractor are covered too, and the principal employer is responsible for paying G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 20.

What the law requires

What goes in

  • You and your employer each pay twelve per cent of your wages S.O. 3582(E).
  • In some industries, such as jute, beedi and brick, the rate is ten per cent s. 16(1)(a) because the twelve per cent notification leaves them out S.O. 3582(E).
  • If your wages are above ₹25,000 a month, the contribution is normally worked out on ₹25,000 S.O. 5109(E) G.S.R. 703(E).
  • You may choose to pay more on wages above that. Your employer need not match it G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 19.
  • Your employer must not cut your wages because of its own contribution s. 124.

Example Your wages are ₹30,000 a month, so contributions stop at ₹25,000. You pay 12% = ₹3,000, and your employer pays ₹3,000. Of your employer’s share, ₹2,083 goes towards your pension and ₹917 stays in your provident fund.

Pension

  • The pension Scheme covers you if you joined the provident fund with wages at or below the ceiling, or were in the earlier pension Schemes G.S.R. 527(E), paragraph 7.
  • A monthly pension needs at least ten years of eligible service G.S.R. 527(E), paragraph 12(1).
  • With less than that, you get a withdrawal benefit instead G.S.R. 527(E), paragraph 13.
  • If you leave before retirement age, you can claim that benefit only thirty-six months after your last contribution fell due, or when you reach that age if earlier G.S.R. 527(E), paragraph 13.

Life cover

  • Your employer pays for this and cannot take it from your wages G.S.R. 526(E), paragraph 8.
  • If you die in service after a continuous twelve months in employment, the amount for your family is at least ₹2,50,000. It depends on your wages and your account balance G.S.R. 526(E), paragraph 21.

Taking out part of your money

  • After twelve months as a member, you can apply for illness, education or marriage, for yourself or your family, or for a home, up to all of your eligible balance G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 46.
  • Twenty-five per cent of your contributions, including your employer’s share and interest, must stay in the account G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 46.
  • Education can be claimed up to ten times, and marriage up to five times G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 46.
  • A home can be claimed up to five times, and special circumstances up to two times in a financial year G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 46.
  • The smallest withdrawal is ₹1,000 G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 46.

Taking out all of your money

  • You can withdraw everything if you retire after the age of fifty-five, if you cannot work again because of permanent and total incapacity, or if you are moving abroad to settle or work G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 49.
  • You can also withdraw everything if you lose your job through retrenchment, or leave under a voluntary retirement scheme G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 49.
  • In other cases, you must have been out of covered employment for a continuous twelve months before applying. That wait does not apply to a woman who resigns to get married G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 49.
  • If you withdraw this way and later join a covered workplace, you start again as a fresh member G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 49.

Changing jobs

  • You can apply on the portal to move your balance to your new job’s account G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 43.

If you die

  • Make your nomination on the portal, and in favour of your family if you have one. A nomination to someone outside your family is then invalid G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 44.
  • Make a fresh nomination when you marry, because the earlier one stops being valid G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 44.
  • With no valid nomination, the money goes to your family in equal shares G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 50.

What to do if it isn’t followed

  • Check your statement on the portal. Tell the Commissioner about any error within three months of the statement being published G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 30.
  • A question about whether you should be a member goes to the Regional Provident Fund Commissioner, and must be raised within five years G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 11.
  • An Authorised Officer can decide what your employer owes, up to five years after the amount fell due s. 125(1).
  • A complete claim must be settled within twenty days. The Commissioner may be charged penal interest of twelve per cent a year for delay without good reason G.S.R. 525(E), paragraph 54.
  • Taking the employer’s share from your wages, or not paying contributions, is an offence s. 133.

Not covered here

  • How the pension amount is worked out G.S.R. 527(E), paragraph 12.
  • Workplaces that run their own provident fund under an exemption s. 143.

What the Labour Ministry says

  • “The employer must contribute 10% of the employee’s wages towards the Provident Fund scheme.” para 6.1, Compliance Handbook for Employers, Feb 2026: official copy · our copy

    Published before S.O. 3582(E) of July 2026, which specifies twelve per cent for most establishments. The Code's own rate is ten per cent, which still applies to those left out.

This is the Ministry's explanation, not the law. Its FAQs and Handbook say the Codes prevail.
